# Attack the Premise

When two or more fixes (or experiments) that share one premise have failed the same gate, suspect the premise, not the fixes.

**Why:** Each failed attempt is evidence against the belief it assumed. A third attempt on the same premise spends effort where the evidence already says the cause is not.

1. **Write the premise down.** One sentence every failed attempt assumed.
2. **Test the premise directly** before the next attempt. Find an observation that would come out differently if the premise were false, and make it. Look at how failures are distributed, not only how large they are.
3. **Follow the evidence.** Contradicted: the cause is elsewhere, look for what the failures share that the premise did not predict. Confirmed: ask the next "why"; earlier fixes may have treated a consequence.

**Stop:** do not start the next attempt before the premise is written down and tested.
